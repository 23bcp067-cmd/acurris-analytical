A rapid mycotoxin test can only tell you about the sample in front of it. Mycotoxins cluster in a small share of kernels, so two samples from the same truck can give very different results. In most mycotoxin programs, sampling causes more error than the test itself.

## Why mycotoxins are hard to sample

Mold grows where moisture collects: at the bottom of a bag, near a leaking roof, in a pocket of damaged grain. A lot with a high average contamination may hold most of its toxin in a few percent of the kernels. Grab one handful and you may miss them entirely, or pick up the worst of them.

Indian feed ingredients also tend to carry more than one toxin at a time. A survey of 540 feed and raw material samples from across India found more than one mycotoxin in 95 percent of them ([survey report](https://benisonmedia.com/a-comprehensive-survey-of-mycotoxin-prevalence-in-india-2017-18/)). A program that tests only aflatoxin can miss a DON or fumonisin problem.

## A sampling routine for the mill gate

**Take many small increments.** Draw increments from across the whole lot: different bags, different depths, different points on the truck. A probe that reaches the bottom of bulk loads helps. More small increments beat a few large ones.

**Combine and mix.** Pool the increments into one aggregate sample and mix it well.

**Grind the whole aggregate.** Grind all of it, not a portion, to a fine and even particle size. Grinding spreads the contaminated kernels through the sample.

**Subsample after grinding.** Take your test portion from the ground, mixed material. Keep a retained portion in a sealed, labeled bag in case you need to retest or send it for confirmation.

EU Regulation 401/2006 sets out sampling plans by lot size and is a useful reference even where it does not apply legally. Your technical team or ours can adapt it to your intake volumes.

## What to test, by ingredient

| Ingredient | Toxins to screen first |
|---|---|
| Maize | Aflatoxins, fumonisins, DON, zearalenone |
| DDGS | Aflatoxins, DON, zearalenone, fumonisins. Ethanol production concentrates toxins from the grain, roughly threefold |
| Groundnut cake | Aflatoxins |
| Cottonseed and other oilcakes | Aflatoxins |
| Wheat and wheat bran | DON, zearalenone |
| Finished feed | The toxins found in its main ingredients |

## Screening and confirmation

Use rapid kits to screen every incoming lot against your acceptance limits. Pick qualitative kits when you only need accept or reject at one limit. Pick semi-quantitative or quantitative kits when you grade lots, adjust price by contamination, or blend.

Send presumptive positives and results close to your limit to a lab for confirmation by HPLC or LC-MS/MS. Keep both results on file with the lot details.

## Matrix effects

Oilcakes, DDGS and finished feed carry fats, pigments and binders that can interfere with rapid tests. Before you rely on a kit for a new ingredient, test it on a few samples with known results, or ask your supplier for data on that matrix.

## Records that pay off

Log every lot: supplier, ingredient, date, quantity, toxin results, and the decision you made. Over a season, these records show you which suppliers and regions bring risk, and they back your rejections when a supplier disputes them.
